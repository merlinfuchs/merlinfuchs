The camera does not show you anything. That is the entire feature. You press the shutter, the
mechanism makes a sound that is very slightly too loud, and then you carry on walking with no
idea whether you got it.

![a grey cat sitting in window light, looking straight down the lens](/cats/portrait.svg#4-5 "worth the other thirty-five")

## The limit does the work

Thirty-six is small enough to notice. By frame thirty you are choosing rather than collecting,
and the choosing is the part I actually enjoy.

- One roll per outing, usually
- Two if I am somewhere I will not be again
- Expired stock when the light does not matter
- The good stock stays in the bag for months, unused, on purpose

> Digital gives you every frame you took. Film gives you the ones you meant.

![a contact sheet of six cat portraits, still wet](/cats/contact-sheet.svg#3-2 "thirty-six decisions, all at once")

## The evening at the scanner

Developing is chemistry with a timer and it is genuinely relaxing. Scanning is not relaxing;
it is dust, and dust, and one more piece of dust. But it is an evening with no notifications in
it, which is worth more than the photographs.
